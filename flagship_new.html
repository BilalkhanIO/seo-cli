Best Business Credit Cards for New Companies (No Revenue)

Starting a company with little or no revenue doesn’t have to block your access to business credit. Many entrepreneurs need a card to pay for supplies, marketing, or travel before their first sales. This guide explains realistic card options, practical application strategies, and credit-building steps so you can get a business credit card for a new business with no revenue.

Quick overview: How you can qualify without business income

  • Apply using your personal credit (Social Security number) as a sole proprietor. Issuers often base the decision on personal credit history if the business has no revenue or EIN history.
  • Use secured business cards that accept a cash deposit as the credit line. These are specifically designed for applicants without established business credit or income.
  • Consider corporate cards tailored to startups (like Brex or Ramp) — some accept new companies with no revenue if you have sufficient personal liquidity or funding, though requirements vary.
  • Leverage personal cards for business spending initially, then transition to business cards once your business profile strengthens.

Best card types and specific card options

Below are practical categories and representative card options that are most accessible to startups and sole proprietors with little or no business revenue.

1. Business cards that approve based on personal credit

These are the most common route for new business owners. Cards evaluate your personal credit score, income (personal or business), and credit history.

  • Chase Ink Business Preferred / Ink Business Cash — Good rewards and welcome offers. Chase is strict about recent account openings and requires solid personal credit. Apply as a sole proprietor using your SSN.
  • Capital One Spark Classic for Business — Easier approval if personal credit isn’t perfect (but still expect a fair credit score requirement). Good for basic business needs.
  • American Express Blue Business Cash — Flexible purchasing power and Amex’s small business features. Amex considers personal creditworthiness for new businesses.

Tip: When applying as a sole proprietor, list “sole proprietor” as the business type and put your name as the business owner. Use your SSN when the form asks for tax ID if you don’t have an EIN yet.

2. Secured business credit cards

If you have weak credit or no credit history, secured business cards are reliable. They require a deposit equal to the credit line, minimizing issuer risk.

  • Secured business card options — Fewer mainstream banks offer secured business cards than secured personal cards, but community banks and online issuers occasionally provide them. Check local bank offerings or credit unions if national issuers decline you.
  • Advantages — Easier approval, builds business-specific credit records if the issuer reports to business credit bureaus.

3. Corporate/startup cards that can work with no revenue

New fintech corporate cards may approve startups with little or no revenue depending on other signals like personal credit, company funding, or bank balances.

  • Brex — Known for startup-friendly underwriting. Brex historically approved companies without personal guarantees if founders had sufficient cash in business accounts or venture backing. Policies change, so check current underwriting requirements.
  • Ramp — Offers corporate-style card with built-in expense controls. Ramp typically looks at business banking cash flow and operational metrics rather than traditional revenue figures.

Important: These issuers may require a business bank account, some deposits, or evidence of funding. If you have startup capital (personal savings moved to a business account), that can be sufficient to qualify.

How to apply when your company has no revenue: step-by-step

Follow these steps to maximize approval chances:

  1. Decide the structure and tax ID — If you’re a sole proprietor, you can apply with your SSN. If you have an LLC or corporation, get an EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS. Even without revenue, an EIN and business bank account make you look more legitimate.
  2. Open a business bank account — Move personal startup capital into a business checking account. Issuers like to see funds and transactions, which can substitute for ongoing revenue.
  3. Check your personal credit — Pull your credit report and fix major errors. Most issuers will consider your personal FICO/credit score for a business application when your business has no revenue.
  4. Prepare documentation — Have your EIN (if available), business name and address, personal SSN, and bank statements handy. Some issuers may request projected revenue or expected monthly spend.
  5. Start with more accessible cards — If you have limited credit history, apply for a secured business card or a business card known for easier approvals. Avoid immediately applying to the strictest issuers.
  6. Be honest on the application — Report your actual revenue (even $0 if that’s the case). Misrepresenting income can lead to account closure or fraud flags.
  7. Use the card responsibly — Keep utilization low, pay on time, and gradually increase spending to build a track record. Consider adding yourself as an authorized user on an established business card to build history.

Practical tips and examples

  • Example: Sole proprietor with strong personal credit — If you have a 720+ FICO score but no business revenue, apply for business cards that underwrite on personal credit (Chase Ink, Amex). Use SSN and projected revenue fields to show expected monthly spend.
  • Example: Early-stage founder with savings but no revenue — Move savings to a business checking account, then try fintech corporate cards (Brex, Ramp) that consider bank balances. Alternatively, apply for a business card using personal credit.
  • Example: Founder with poor credit or thin history — Opt for secured business cards or get a co-signer/authorized user on someone else’s account temporarily while building credit.
  • Leverage vendor credit — Start with vendor accounts that extend net-30 terms (e.g., suppliers, trade vendors) to build a credit history and then apply for a card after 3–6 months of payments.

Building business credit when there’s no revenue

Getting a card is only the start. Use these steps to build a trackable business credit profile:

  • Open accounts with vendors that report to business credit bureaus (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business).
  • Request a DUNS number and make timely vendor payments to establish a PAYDEX score with Dun & Bradstreet.
  • Keep separate business finances—use the business card for business purchases only and pay balances on time.
  • Gradually add authorized users and apply for small increases or new cards as revenue begins.

Common mistakes and misconceptions to avoid

  • Misrepresenting income — Listing false revenue can lead to denial or account closure. Use accurate numbers or conservative projections.
  • Assuming revenue is always required — Many issuers underwrite using personal credit or bank balances. If you’re a sole proprietor, your SSN and personal income matter more than business revenue early on.
  • Applying to every card at once — Multiple hard inquiries in a short period can hurt approval odds. Space applications and target ones that match your profile.
  • Mixing personal and business finances — It’s tempting at launch, but mixing accounts makes underwriting murkier and undermines business credit-building.
  • Expecting instant high credit limits — New businesses typically get modest limits at first. Build credit history to qualify for higher lines later.

Checklist before you apply

  • Do you have a personal credit score of ~650+? If not, consider secured options or credit improvement.
  • Do you have an EIN and business bank account? If not, consider applying as a sole proprietor with your SSN or open an EIN quickly.
  • Have you prepared recent personal bank statements or evidence of startup capital?
  • Have you checked which bureaus the issuer uses (personal vs. business) and their typical requirements?
  • Are you ready to keep business spending and payments separate and timely?

When to consider personal credit cards for business use

In many early-stage startups, the fastest path is to use a personal card for business expenses temporarily. Choose a card with robust purchase protections and track expenses carefully for tax and accounting purposes. Plan to transition to a business card within 6–12 months to build distinct business credit.

Final thoughts: realistic expectations and next steps

Getting the best business credit cards for new business with no revenue is very possible if you apply strategically. The most reliable routes are:

  • Applying with your personal credit as a sole proprietor
  • Using secured business cards if you have limited credit
  • Leveraging fintech corporate cards if you have business bank account balances or funding

Start by organizing business paperwork (EIN, bank account), checking your personal credit, and choosing issuers that match your profile. Use small, consistent charges and pay on time to build business credit quickly. Avoid misrepresentation and too many applications at once. With a careful approach, you’ll secure a card that helps your startup grow even before revenue arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Can you get a business credit card with no revenue?

Yes. Most issuers approve based on your personal credit and income, so you can enter $0 business revenue and still qualify by signing a personal guarantee. Major issuers confirm this.

What credit score do you need for a business credit card with no revenue?

A personal FICO score of about 670 or higher is the practical bar for most personal-guarantee business cards. Secured business cards accept lower or limited scores.

Can an LLC with no income get a credit card?

Yes — a brand-new LLC with no income can apply. Approval rests on the owner's personal credit and income, not the LLC's sales, and you'll typically sign a personal guarantee.

Do you have to enter revenue on a business credit card application?

You enter your business revenue, but $0 (or a realistic projection) is acceptable for a startup. Issuers weigh your personal income and credit far more heavily at this stage.

Can you get a business card with no personal guarantee?

Rarely for brand-new companies. No-personal-guarantee cards (startup/corporate cards) usually require meaningful cash in a linked business bank account or investor funding.
