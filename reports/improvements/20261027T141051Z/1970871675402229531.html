Best Business Credit Cards for New LLCs (No Credit?)

Forming an LLC is an important step in separating your personal finances from your business. But if your newly formed LLC has little or no credit history, you may be unsure how to get a business credit card that will help you manage cash flow and build business credit. This guide explains the practical options for the best business credit cards for new LLC with no credit history, how to qualify, and step-by-step strategies to build credit quickly and responsibly.

Why a business card matters for a new LLC

  • Separates personal and business expenses for taxes and bookkeeping.
  • Helps build a credit profile for the business, unlocking better financing later.
  • Provides short-term cash flow and benefits like rewards, purchase protections, and vendor payment terms.
  • Makes spending transparent for partners, investors, and accountants.

But a new LLC without a business credit history faces hurdles. Issuers want to evaluate risk. That means you’ll need to know the realistic card types and the application steps that work for startups with no credit.

Quick overview: Your realistic card choices

If your LLC has limited or no business credit, these are the most common and practical card categories to consider:

  • Secured business credit cards: Require a cash deposit that becomes your credit line. These are the most reliable option for an LLC with no credit history.
  • Small-business starter cards that accept personal guarantees: Many business cards can be approved based on the owner’s personal credit score and income. Good when a founder has fair-to-good personal credit.
  • Business cards from fintech/corporate card providers that underwrite on revenue: Some modern providers approve accounts based on business bank account activity and revenue instead of long credit history — useful for revenue-generating startups.
  • Corporate cards for new businesses with limited credit history: These are sometimes available if the company has a corporate relationship with a bank or an investor backing, but traditional corporate cards usually need established business credit or a guarantee.

How to get a business credit card for a new LLC: step-by-step

1. Decide whether you’ll use a personal guarantee

Most business credit cards require a personal guarantee from an owner, meaning the owner is personally responsible if the business can’t pay. If you’re willing to provide a personal guarantee and you have decent personal credit, you’ll have many more options. If you want to avoid a personal guarantee, you’ll be limited to cards from providers that underwrite to business revenue or secured business cards that accept a deposit.

2. Gather documents and set up basic business infrastructure

  • Employer Identification Number (EIN) — obtain from the IRS.
  • Business bank account opened in the LLC’s name.
  • Articles of Organization/Certificate of Formation and operating agreement.
  • Business phone number, email, and website (even a simple one).

Having these items ready signals legitimacy and helps issuers verify your company quickly.

3. Choose the right card type for your situation

Match the card type to your credit status:

  • If you have little or no personal and business credit: look into secured business credit cards or corporate cards from fintech providers that approve based on bank account history.
  • If you have fair-to-excellent personal credit: many starter business cards accept a personal guarantee; you'll likely qualify for cards with more perks and higher limits.
  • If your business already has some revenue and a stable bank history: consider card products that evaluate cash flow instead of credit history (expense-management cards and some corporate cards).

4. Apply strategically

When applying, be prepared to:

  • Provide EIN, business bank account info, and formation documents.
  • Supply personal information and consent to a personal credit pull if required.
  • Disclose business revenue and time in business — some issuers will approve young businesses with sufficient monthly deposits.

Avoid applying to multiple cards at once — too many hard inquiries can hurt your personal credit and reduce approval odds.

5. Use the card to build business credit

Once you have a card, treat it like a credit-building tool:

  • Keep utilization low (under 30% of the limit).
  • Pay on time or early — payment history is the most important factor.
  • Ask the issuer to report your account to business credit bureaus (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business). Not all cards report automatically.
  • Establish multiple trade lines over time: vendor accounts, net-30 vendors, and small loans help diversify business credit.

Types of cards explained (pros and cons)

Secured business credit cards for new LLC

How they work: You deposit cash as collateral, and the lender sets your credit limit equal to or slightly higher than that deposit.

  • Pros: Easier approval for businesses with no history; builds credit when issuer reports; predictable limit based on deposit.
  • Cons: Requires upfront cash; may have fewer perks and lower limits; not all secured cards report to business credit bureaus.

Business cards that accept a personal guarantee

How they work: The owner pledges personal responsibility for the account. Issuers use the owner’s personal credit score to qualify.

  • Pros: Access to mainstream business cards with rewards and higher limits; can be obtained by owners with good personal credit even if the LLC is new.
  • Cons: Risks owner’s personal credit; can be harder to separate personal liability.

Corporate/fintech cards that underwrite on revenue

How they work: Some modern card providers look at business bank deposits, recurring revenue, and cash flow instead of just credit history. They may offer cards without a personal guarantee to qualifying companies.

  • Pros: No or limited personal guarantee; approval based on business performance; often include spend controls and integrations for scaling startups.
  • Cons: Usually require consistent deposits and a business bank relationship; may limit options for very early-stage companies with no revenue.

Best starter business cards for small business owners — practical examples

Rather than a one-size-fits-all “best” card, choose based on your situation:

  • If you have low personal credit and little business history: start with a secured business credit card from a reputable bank that reports to business credit agencies.
  • If you have good personal credit: apply for beginner-friendly business cards that accept personal guarantees — these often include simple cash-back or low-fee options.
  • If your startup already has monthly revenue: explore modern corporate/fintech cards that evaluate cash flow and may not require a personal guarantee.

When comparing specific offers, look for annual fees, reporting to business bureaus, introductory APRs, rewards that match your spending, and whether the card requires or avoids a personal guarantee.

Common mistakes and misconceptions to avoid

  • Mistake: Applying for many cards at once. That causes multiple hard inquiries and can lower approval odds.
  • Misconception: “I can’t get a business card until the LLC has years of history.” Not true — secured cards, personal-guarantee cards, and fintech options exist precisely for new businesses.
  • Mistake: Not separating personal and business expenses. Mixing them makes it harder to build clean business credit and complicates taxes.
  • Misconception: “Business cards don't affect personal credit.” Many business cards do require a personal guarantee and can appear on personal credit reports if the company defaults.
  • Mistake: Focusing only on perks. For a new LLC, the priority should be credit reporting, approval odds, and manageable terms rather than high-end rewards.

Checklist: Before you apply

  • Obtain an EIN and open a business bank account.
  • Register your business with state and local authorities.
  • Gather personal ID and business formation documents.
  • Check your personal credit report and fix errors.
  • Decide whether you’ll provide a personal guarantee.
  • Choose the card category that fits your credit and revenue profile.
  • Confirm the issuer reports to business credit bureaus.

How to build business credit quickly after approval

Getting a card is only the start. To create a strong business credit profile:

  • Use the card for regular, planned business expenses — payroll, supplies, subscriptions — and pay the statement in full each month.
  • Keep utilization low and avoid maxing out the card, even if you have a large limit.
  • Request credit limit increases occasionally as revenue grows; higher limits with the same balance lower utilization.
  • Establish vendor credit lines with suppliers who report to business bureaus (trade credit). Net-30 vendors can be a fast way to add tradelines.
  • Monitor business credit scores (Dun & Bradstreet PAYDEX, Experian Business, Equifax Business) and address inaccuracies quickly.

When to transition from starter cards to traditional business financing

As your LLC establishes history and revenue, aim to graduate to cards and loans that don’t require personal guarantees and that offer better terms for established businesses. Milestones to watch for:

  • Consistent revenue and monthly cash flow over 6–12 months.
  • Multiple business tradelines reporting positive payment history.
  • A business credit score that meets lender thresholds (varies by bureau).
  • Strong banking relationships or investor backing.

Summary — Which option is right for your new LLC?

If you’re searching for the best business credit cards for new LLC with no credit history, pick the route that matches your current situation:

  • Very little personal or business credit: start with a secured business credit card that reports to business bureaus.
  • Good personal credit but new LLC: apply for starter business cards that accept personal guarantees to get rewards and higher limits.
  • Revenue-generating startup: explore fintech and corporate card providers that underwrite on cash flow and business banking history.

Whatever path you choose, focus first on building reliable payment history and ensuring that issuers report to the business credit bureaus. Over time, responsible use will open doors to unsecured corporate cards, higher limits, and traditional business financing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a new LLC get a credit card?

Yes. A new LLC can get a business credit card based on the owner's personal credit and income, typically with a personal guarantee — business revenue isn't required.

Do you need revenue for a business credit card as an LLC?

No. You can enter $0 or projected revenue; issuers weigh your personal income and credit far more heavily for a new LLC.

Does an LLC need an EIN for a business credit card?

A single-member LLC can sometimes apply with an SSN, but an EIN is free, common for business cards, and required if you have employees.

Do you have to sign a personal guarantee?

For most new LLCs, yes. A personal guarantee makes you personally responsible if the business can't pay, which is how issuers manage the risk.

Does a business credit card build business credit?

It can, if the card reports to business credit bureaus. Pay on time and keep utilization low to build a strong business credit file.
