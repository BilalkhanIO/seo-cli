Best Invoicing Apps for Freelancers With Expense Tracking

Freelancers need two things from their finance tools: fast, professional invoices to get paid and reliable expense tracking to simplify taxes and profit analysis. If you're searching for the best invoicing app for freelancers with expense tracking free (or under $10/month), this guide walks through the top budget-friendly options, how they compare, and exactly how to choose and set one up so you can invoice clients and manage receipts without blowing your budget.

What freelancers really need from invoicing + expense tracking

  • Simple invoice creation and delivery (PDF + email)
  • Automated reminders and basic payment acceptance (card, ACH, PayPal)
  • Expense capture: receipt photo upload or email receipts, and categorization
  • Basic reporting for profit/loss and tax-ready expense lists
  • Low or no monthly cost, ideally free or under $10/month
  • Mobile app or easy receipt upload for on-the-go contractors

Big accounting suites often charge more for receipt scanning and separate add-ons. Below are practical, low-cost alternatives that combine invoicing and expense tracking in ways that work for independent contractors, self-employed creators, and consultants.

Top picks: Best invoicing apps for freelancers under $10 month (or free)

1) Wave — Free invoicing + built-in receipts

Why it stands out: Wave is one of the few full-featured small-business tools that offers free invoicing and integrated bookkeeping. You can create unlimited professional invoices, send automated reminders, and attach receipts to expense entries.

  • Expense tracking: Upload receipt photos or email receipts; categorize expenses for tax time.
  • Invoicing: Unlimited free invoices and clients; customizable templates.
  • Payments: Credit card and bank payment processing are available (transaction fees apply).
  • Best for: Solo freelancers and contractors who want a no-cost solution with bookkeeping.
  • Drawback: Some advanced features (like payroll or advanced reporting) are paid add-ons.

2) Invoice Ninja — Free plan + affordable paid tier

Why it stands out: Invoice Ninja combines invoicing, proposals, time tracking, and expense management in a compact app. The hosted free tier supports many freelancers; the first paid plan is affordable for teams or heavier use.

  • Expense tracking: Add expenses and attach receipt images; track vendor info and tags.
  • Invoicing: Branded invoices, recurring billing, auto-reminders on paid plans.
  • Pricing: There's a robust free plan; paid hosted plans start in the low range per month.
  • Best for: Freelancers who want optional upgrades (client portals, more automations) but prefer a strong free base.
  • Drawback: UX can feel more technical compared with consumer-oriented apps.

3) Zoho Invoice — Powerful free invoicing with expense features

Why it stands out: Zoho Invoice is part of the Zoho suite and offers a generous free invoicing product with expense logging and receipt attachment. It’s intuitive and integrates with other Zoho products if you scale later.

  • Expense tracking: Record expenses, attach receipts, and convert billable expenses into invoices.
  • Invoicing: Custom templates, recurring invoices, and client portal options.
  • Best for: Self-employed users who want a polished UI and optional integrations without a big cost.
  • Drawback: Payment processing and advanced business features may involve transaction fees or upgrades.

4) ZipBooks — Simple free invoicing + light expense tracking

Why it stands out: ZipBooks provides clean invoice templates, easy bookkeeping basics, and expense entry on its free tier. It's a good lightweight alternative if you want a tidy dashboard and painless setup.

  • Expense tracking: Manually add expenses and upload receipts; categorize for reports.
  • Invoicing: Unlimited invoices on free plan; simple reporting and customer management.
  • Best for: Freelancers who prioritize an easy UI and quick onboarding.
  • Drawback: Some reporting and bank reconciliation features require paid tiers.

5) PayPal Invoicing — Free invoicing; pay-per-transaction

Why it stands out: If you already rely on PayPal for payments, PayPal Invoicing lets you send invoices for free and capture payments quickly. Expense tracking is more manual—use PayPal transaction history or link with a separate receipt app—but combined solutions keep costs near zero.

  • Expense tracking: Not native—use exported transaction lists or pair with a free expense app.
  • Invoicing: Create, send, and track invoices for free; client pays with PayPal or card (fees apply).
  • Best for: Contractors who prioritize payment speed and are okay handling receipts separately.
  • Drawback: Limited native expense management; best combined with another free receipt capture tool.

How to choose the right app for your freelance business

Use this short checklist to match a tool to your workflow and budget.

  • Do you need full bookkeeping or just expense lists for taxes? If just expenses, a simple free invoice app with receipt upload (Wave, Zoho Invoice) is enough.
  • Do you want mobile receipt capture? Make sure the app’s mobile experience is smooth—Wave and Invoice Ninja offer mobile-friendly upload.
  • Do you want payment processing inside the same app? If yes, check transaction fees and supported payment methods.
  • How many clients or invoices per month? Some free plans cap features; if you bill dozens of clients, confirm limits.
  • Do you need integrations (PayPal, Stripe, bank import)? Integrations can save manual data entry and reduce errors.

Step-by-step: Setting up a cheap invoicing + expense workflow

Here’s a practical setup you can implement in under an hour using a free or <$10/month app.

  1. Choose an app: Pick one from the list above based on mobile capture, payment needs, and UI preference.
  2. Create your account and add business details: logo, address, tax ID (if needed), and default payment terms.
  3. Connect payment processing: Link Stripe, PayPal, or bank transfer options so clients can pay directly from invoices.
  4. Set up expense categories: Create categories like Travel, Supplies, Subcontractors, Meals, and Software for tax time.
  5. Install mobile app and test receipt capture: Snap a receipt, attach it to an expense, and mark whether it’s billable to a client.
  6. Create an invoice template: Save a template with your branding, payment terms, and itemized rates for common services.
  7. Automate reminders and late fees: Turn on reminders to reduce follow-ups; add a modest late fee if desired.
  8. Run month-end reports: Export expenses and invoice income to prepare quarterly taxes or hand off to an accountant.

Practical tips and examples

  • Example workflow: For each client project, create a project folder in the app. Mark expenses as billable when they relate to that project. At month-end, convert billable expenses to an invoice to ensure you’re reimbursed.
  • Receipts: Photograph receipts immediately. Most apps attach images to expenses; keep descriptive notes (e.g., “Client lunch with X — project Y”).
  • Tax time: Use expense categories consistently. That saves hours when you or your accountant prepares Schedule C or other tax forms.
  • Mix-and-match: If an app lacks a feature, use a small dedicated expense scanner (free iOS/Android apps) and import CSVs into your invoicing tool monthly.
  • Backups: Export invoices and expenses quarterly as CSV or PDF for redundancy.

Common mistakes and misconceptions to avoid

  • Thinking a “free” app means no fees: Even free invoicing tools usually charge payment processing fees when clients pay by card. Factor that into your rates.
  • Skipping receipt photos: Trying to reconstruct expenses later wastes time and can increase audit risk. Snap receipts promptly.
  • Not categorizing expenses: Unsorted expenses make tax prep painful. Create and use consistent categories from day one.
  • Keeping everything in separate apps: Using one tool for invoicing and expense tracking reduces reconciliation work and errors.
  • Choosing features you don’t use: Don’t pay for advanced workflows (project management, payroll) you’ll never use. Stick to invoicing + expense tracking if that’s all you need.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing app with expense tracking for freelancers?

An app that captures receipts, categorizes expenses, and exports a tax summary alongside professional invoicing — so your income and deductions live in one place.

Why track expenses inside your invoicing app?

Keeping invoices and expenses together means every deductible cost is logged as you go, so Schedule C and quarterly taxes are quick instead of a year-end scramble.

Can invoicing apps track mileage?

Some do, either automatically via your phone's GPS or by manual entry — useful for claiming the IRS standard mileage deduction if you drive for work.

Do freelancers really need expense tracking?

If you claim business deductions, yes — the IRS expects records. Automated tracking maximizes legitimate deductions and protects you if you're ever audited.

Is free expense-tracking software good enough?

Free tiers handle basic receipt logging and invoicing for low-volume freelancers. Paid plans add automation, reports, and accounting sync as your work grows.
