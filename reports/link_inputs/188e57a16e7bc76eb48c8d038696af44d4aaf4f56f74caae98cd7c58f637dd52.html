Figuring out how to do self-employed taxes is one of the most stressful parts of freelancing — but it comes down to a few repeatable steps. This guide walks through what you owe, how to set money aside, when to pay, and which forms you file, so you can handle self-employment tax with confidence. What is self-employment tax?

When you're self-employed, you pay self-employment (SE) tax to cover Social Security and Medicare — the parts an employer would normally split with you. The SE tax rate is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on your net self-employment earnings, up to the annual Social Security wage cap (which the IRS updates each year). You also pay regular income tax on top of that.

Step 1: Track income and expenses all year

You're taxed on net profit, not gross income, so tracking deductible business expenses directly lowers your tax bill. Keep records of income and expenses as you go — using a good invoicing and expense tool and a separate business account makes this painless at filing time.

Step 2: Set aside money for taxes

A common rule of thumb is to set aside 25–30% of every payment in a separate account for taxes, so a big invoice doesn't become a tax surprise. Freelancers with irregular income especially benefit from moving that percentage aside the moment they get paid.

Step 3: Pay quarterly estimated taxes

Unlike employees, the self-employed usually must pay estimated taxes four times a year rather than once in April. The typical deadlines are mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January. Missing them can trigger penalties, so mark them on your calendar and pay through the IRS website.

Step 4: File the right forms

At tax time you report business profit on Schedule C and calculate SE tax on Schedule SE, filed with your Form 1040. Helpfully, you can deduct half of your self-employment tax and, if eligible, contributions to a self-employed retirement plan — both reduce your taxable income.

Common self-employed deductions

Typical deductions include the home-office deduction, business software and subscriptions, a portion of your phone and internet, business travel and mileage, and health insurance premiums in some cases. Keep receipts and only claim genuine business expenses.

Frequently asked questions

How do I do my self-employed taxes?

Track income and deductible expenses all year, set aside 25-30% of income, pay quarterly estimated taxes, then file Schedule C (profit) and Schedule SE (self-employment tax) with your Form 1040. Confirm specifics with the IRS or a tax professional.

How much tax do self-employed people pay?

You pay 15.3% self-employment tax on net profit (Social Security + Medicare) plus regular income tax. Your total rate depends on your income bracket and deductions.

How much should I save for self-employed taxes?

A common rule of thumb is 25-30% of each payment set aside in a separate account. Higher earners may need to save more; a tax professional can give you a precise figure.

When are self-employed taxes due?

Estimated taxes are generally due four times a year (mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January), with the annual return filed by the April deadline.

What can self-employed people deduct?

Common deductions include home office, software, a share of phone/internet, business travel and mileage, and half of your self-employment tax. Only claim genuine business expenses and keep records.
