An unpaid freelance invoice usually needs a calm, written follow-up, not a confrontation. To follow up on an unpaid freelance invoice, check the invoice details first, send a short polite reminder restating the invoice, the amount, and the due date, give one clear new date to pay by, and keep every message in writing — so the paper trail is clear if the payment still does not come. This is general information, not legal or financial advice, and local contract and tax rules differ.

Key takeaways:

  • Check the invoice details before you chase it — date, amount, and due date.
  • Send a short, polite reminder first; most late payments are oversights.
  • Give one clear new payment date in every follow-up.
  • Keep every message in writing and save each one.
  • Escalate in steps: reminder, firm notice, then formal options — do not jump straight to threats.

Check the invoice before you chase it

Before you write to the client, check the invoice itself: the invoice number, the amount, the date it was sent, and the due date you agreed. Also check whether the client confirmed receipt, and whether the payment terms in your agreement say anything about late payment. A follow-up that quotes the wrong amount or the wrong date weakens your position, and a quick check often reveals a simple cause — an invoice sent to the wrong address, or a due date that has not actually passed. Send a short reminder first

Most late payments are oversights, not refusals, so the first message should be short and polite. Restate the invoice number, the amount, and the original due date, attach the invoice again, and ask the client to confirm when payment will be made. Do not apologise for asking, and do not add new charges or threats in the first reminder. One short message, sent to the same person who approved the work, is usually enough. If you are unsure how to price the work you are chasing, the method for working out a floor rate is in How to Price Freelance Work Without Undercharging on the same blog.

Give one clear payment date

Every follow-up should name one date by which payment is expected. A vague request to pay "soon" is easy to ignore; a specific date gives the client something concrete to act on and gives you a clear point to follow up again if it passes. State the date in the message, and say what you will do next if it passes — for example, that you will send a formal notice. Keep the tone professional: the date is the pressure, not the language. Escalate in steps, in writing

If the reminder and the new date pass without payment, escalate in steps. Send a firmer notice restating the amount, the dates, and the work delivered, and ask for payment by a final date. After that, the options depend on your contract and where you are: a formal letter before action, a debt collection service, or a small-claims process are the usual routes, and each has its own rules and costs, so get local advice before you start one. Prevent the next late payment

Once this invoice is settled, make the next one harder to delay. Put the payment terms in writing before work starts — the amount, the due date, and what happens if payment is late — and send the invoice the day the work is delivered, not at the end of the month. Ask the client to confirm receipt of the invoice, and consider asking for a deposit or milestone payments on larger projects, so a single late payment never puts a whole project at risk. FAQ

How long should I wait before following up?

Follow up the day after the due date passes. A short reminder right after the due date is normal business practice, not pushiness, and it catches oversights early.

What should the first reminder say?

The invoice number, the amount, the original due date, and a request to confirm when payment will be made — plus the invoice attached again. Keep it to a few lines.

What if the client says they never received the invoice?

Resend it in the same message, ask them to confirm receipt, and note the new due date in writing. Then check that you have the right contact and address for future invoices.

What if the client keeps promising to pay?

Ask for a specific payment date in writing, and follow up on that date if it passes. Repeated promises without a date are a sign to move to a firmer written notice.

Can I charge interest on a late invoice?

It depends on your contract and local law. Check what your agreement says first, and look up the rules where you and the client are based — in the UK, the GOV.UK late commercial payments page explains them. Do not add interest your contract does not allow.

Is this legal advice?

No. This is general information for US, UK, Canada, and EU readers. Local contract, tax, and debt-recovery rules differ, so get local professional advice for complex or high-value invoices.
