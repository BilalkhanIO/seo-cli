What new LLC owners need from a business checking account

When your primary goal is finding the best business checking account for new LLC with no monthly fee and easy online setup, consider these attributes:

  • No monthly maintenance fee — clear and non-negotiable if you want to avoid recurring costs.
  • Online account opening — minimal paperwork and the ability to finish setup remotely.
  • Transfer capabilities — free ACH transfers, bill pay, and external transfers to avoid extra fees.
  • Transaction limits and fees — look for unlimited or generous transaction counts and low debit/ATM fees.
  • Integration & tools — bookkeeping, payment processing, invoicing, and accounting integrations save time.
  • Documentation requirements — easy onboarding for a newly formed LLC (EIN, Articles of Organization, operating agreement, owner ID).

How to open a business checking account for a new LLC online: step-by-step

Opening is usually straightforward if you have the right documents. Follow this checklist to finish the process in one session with minimal delays.

  1. Confirm your LLC’s paperwork — Articles of Organization (or Certificate of Formation) and Employer Identification Number (EIN). Some banks allow single-member LLCs to use the owner’s SSN, but an EIN is recommended.
  2. Prepare owner ID and contact details — government-issued ID (driver’s license or passport), email, phone number, and home/business address.
  3. Operating Agreement and Ownership Information — many banks request your LLC operating agreement and a list of members/owners with ownership percentages.
  4. Decide account signers — who will have online access and who will be authorized signers on checks.
  5. Start the online application — choose the bank that matches your needs, select the business checking product, and complete the digital form while uploading documents.
  6. Fund the account — most banks require an initial deposit (often low or $0 for no-fee accounts). Transfer from a personal account, wire, or mobile deposit where supported.
  7. Set up digital tools — enable online banking, connect accounting software, set up ACH for payroll or inbound payments, order debit cards, and configure alerts.

Practical tips and examples

  • If you’re a single-member LLC: Novo and BlueVine are very friendly for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs and typically do not require in-person visits.
  • If you need developer tools or multiple subaccounts: Mercury’s subaccount and API capabilities help startups split funds (e.g., payroll vs. operating expenses) without multiple bank relationships.
  • Want free transfers: Confirm that the account includes free ACH transfers and outgoing domestic ACH; some banks charge for outgoing wires but not ACH.
  • ATM access: Online-only banks may use ATM networks and reimburse fees up to a limit — verify the reimbursement policy if cash access matters.
  • Integration considerations: If you use Stripe, Square, QuickBooks, or a payroll provider, check native integrations to save manual uploads.

Common mistakes and misconceptions to avoid

  • Misconception: “No monthly fee means zero fees.” Many no-monthly-fee accounts still charge for wire transfers, international transactions, or cash deposits. Read the fine print for per-item fees.
  • Mistake: Using a personal bank for business. Mixing personal and business funds can hurt liability protection and complicate bookkeeping and taxes. Open a dedicated LLC business checking account as soon as possible.
  • Mistake: Choosing solely for APY. High APY is nice but not at the expense of banking functionality. Prioritize free transfers, integrations, and easy onboarding.
  • Mistake: Not verifying online onboarding rules for your specific LLC type. Foreign-owned LLCs, multi-member LLCs, or series LLCs sometimes face additional documentation or are ineligible for some online-only banks.
  • Mistake: Overlooking transfer limits and hold times. Some banks place holds on large deposits or limit same-day outbound transfers for new accounts. Check policies if you expect high volumes early on.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best business checking account for a new LLC?

One with no monthly fee, no minimum balance, free ACH, and a debit card — so a pre-revenue LLC keeps costs at zero while separating business funds.

What documents does a new LLC need to open a bank account?

Typically your EIN, articles of organization, an operating agreement (for multi-member LLCs), and photo ID for the owners.

Does an LLC need an EIN to open a bank account?

Yes. Unlike sole proprietors, LLCs generally must provide an EIN — it's free from the IRS and takes minutes to obtain online.

Can I use a personal account for my LLC?

You shouldn't. Mixing personal and LLC funds can undermine the liability protection an LLC provides and complicates taxes and bookkeeping.

Single-member vs multi-member LLC — does it change account opening?

Multi-member LLCs are more likely to be asked for an operating agreement; both need an EIN and formation documents.
