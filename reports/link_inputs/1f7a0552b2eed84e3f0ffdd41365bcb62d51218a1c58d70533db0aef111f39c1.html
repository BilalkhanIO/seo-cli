Best Invoicing Software for Freelancers Under $10/Month

Freelancers with tight budgets need invoicing tools that are affordable, simple, and professional. Whether you’re a sole proprietor, independent contractor, or side-hustle freelancer, the right invoicing software under $10/month can save time, speed up payments, and help you look polished to clients. This guide reviews the best invoicing software for freelancers under $10/month, compares free vs low-cost options, shares setup tips, and points out common mistakes to avoid.

What freelancers need from invoicing software

Before picking one of the affordable invoicing apps for freelancers, consider what matters most for your workflow:

  • Easy invoice creation and customization (logo, terms, itemized line items)
  • Multiple invoice delivery options (email, PDF, client portal)
  • Online payments and integration with Stripe/PayPal/Square
  • Client management and recurring invoices
  • Expense tracking and basic reporting for taxes
  • Reliable support and data export (CSV/PDF)
  • Simple pricing model—no unexpected transaction or platform fees

Use this checklist while evaluating invoicing tools under $10 per month for freelancers to make sure the software covers your essentials.

Top picks: Best invoicing software for freelancers under $10/month

Below are practical, budget-conscious options that balance features and affordability. Many are completely free and rely on payment-processing fees only; others offer paid plans at or under $10/month for extra features like client portals, multiple users, or automated reminders.

1. Wave — Best free invoicing software for sole proprietors

Why it’s good: Wave is designed for small businesses and sole proprietors who want a no-cost solution. You can create unlimited invoices, send them by email, and accept payments via credit card or bank transfer (ACH). Wave also offers integrated accounting and receipt scanning, which adds value without recurring costs.

  • Price: Free for invoicing and accounting; payment processing fees apply
  • Key features: Unlimited invoices, customizable templates, basic reporting, integrated accounting
  • Drawbacks: No advanced automation or client portal on the free plan; payment fees charged per transaction

2. Zoho Invoice — Full-featured free tier with automation

Why it’s good: Zoho Invoice has a robust free tier for small freelancers, offering client portals, recurring invoices, time tracking, and automated payment reminders. The interface is clean and integrates well with other Zoho apps if you grow into a broader ecosystem later.

  • Price: Free for many users on the standalone Zoho Invoice plan (check current limits)
  • Key features: Client portal, recurring billing, expense tracking, multi-currency support
  • Drawbacks: Some advanced features are gated behind paid Zoho plans; UI may be feature-rich for very simple users

3. Invoice Ninja — Low-cost plan for growing freelancers

Why it’s good: Invoice Ninja offers a generous free plan and a paid Pro plan that often sits around $10/month. It supports proposals, invoices, client portals, time tracking, and multiple integrations. For freelancers who want a professional client portal and automation but can’t exceed $10/month, Invoice Ninja is a strong contender.

  • Price: Free tier available; Pro plans often start at roughly $10/month (confirm current pricing)
  • Key features: Client portal, recurring invoices, payment gateways, proposals, time tracking
  • Drawbacks: The free plan has limits on features and branding; self-hosting option exists but requires technical setup

4. Square Invoices — Simple, no-monthly-fee invoicing with POS tie-in

Why it’s good: Square Invoices is free to use—no monthly fee—making it ideal for freelancers who need straightforward invoice sending and fast payment via Square. It’s particularly useful if you already use Square for in-person payments.

  • Price: No monthly fee; transaction fees apply when clients pay with card
  • Key features: Simple invoice builder, recurring invoices, estimates, built-in payment processing
  • Drawbacks: Limited advanced accounting features; transaction fees can add up

5. PayPal Invoicing — Cheap invoicing with familiar payments

Why it’s good: If many clients already use PayPal, its invoicing is a lightweight choice—no monthly subscription and easy online payment for clients. PayPal is ideal for freelancers who need simple invoices and fast acceptance by clients internationally.

  • Price: No monthly fee; PayPal transaction fees per payment
  • Key features: Create and send invoices, recurring payments, mobile-friendly
  • Drawbacks: Higher transaction fees for international or credit card payments; limited reporting

How to choose between free and paid (< $10/month) plans

When comparing these affordable invoicing apps for freelancers, follow this decision process:

  1. List must-have features: client portal, recurring invoices, tax/expense tracking, or online payments.
  2. Estimate monthly transaction volume: If you bill a lot and are charged per-transaction, a low monthly fee might reduce overall costs.
  3. Check client expectations: Some clients prefer a portal to view past invoices or make payments—if that matters, prioritize tools with a client portal.
  4. Confirm payment gateway compatibility: Ensure Stripe, PayPal, or Square integrations match your and your clients’ preferences.
  5. Test the free tier first: Many apps let you try features before upgrading. Start free and only pay if you miss automation or limits.

This way you’ll avoid paying for features you don’t need while keeping your process efficient.

Practical setup: How to get started in under 30 minutes

Here’s a simple step-by-step setup you can complete quickly with most inexpensive invoicing tools:

  1. Create an account and verify your email.
  2. Add your business name, logo, contact information, and payment details (Stripe/PayPal/Square).
  3. Set up tax rates and default invoice terms (e.g., Net 15 or Net 30) and a late fee policy if you use one.
  4. Create a client profile with billing address and preferred payment method.
  5. Build an invoice template—add logo, itemized services, rates, and payment instructions.
  6. Send a test invoice to yourself to check the PDF, email content, and payment flow.
  7. Enable automated reminders and recurring invoices if you have retainer clients.

Once set up, use an invoice numbering system and save exported reports periodically for taxes.

Tips for speeding up payments and using cheap invoicing software effectively

  • Offer multiple online payment options to remove friction—card, bank transfer, or PayPal.
  • Set clear payment terms and mention due dates on every invoice.
  • Include a concise description of work and attach deliverables or time logs if relevant.
  • Automate reminders—late fees sometimes motivate quicker payments, but use them sparingly to preserve client relationships.
  • Send invoices promptly: invoices sent within a day of delivery get paid faster than those delayed.
  • Keep a simple “Payment Instructions” section on each invoice listing preferred methods and any processing fees.

Common mistakes and misconceptions to avoid

Mistake: Choosing the cheapest tool without checking payment fees

Many freelancers assume “free” equals low cost. But payment processing fees (often 2.9% + a fixed fee) can make a free platform more expensive per transaction. Calculate projected monthly transaction fees to compare real costs.

Mistake: Overlooking client experience

If your client finds the invoice confusing or the payment process clunky, you risk slower payments. Prioritize clarity and a clean client portal over unnecessary bells and whistles.

Mistake: Skipping data export/backups

Relying only on the cloud without regular exports can cause trouble during audits or if you switch platforms. Export invoices and client lists periodically (CSV/PDF).

Misconception: Paid plans always mean better support

Support quality varies. Some free tools offer solid community help and clear self-service documentation. Check support channels (email, chat, phone) and response expectations before you commit.

  • If you want a low-cost upgrade option that adds portals and proposals: Invoice Ninja is a top pick.
  • If you want minimal setup and fast payments with POS integration: Square Invoices fits well.

Example invoice workflow for a freelancer

Here’s an example use-case for a freelance designer billing $1,200 monthly retainer and occasional project work:

  1. Use Zoho Invoice (free) to create a client profile and enable the client portal.
  2. Create a recurring invoice for the $1,200 retainer, set to auto-send on the 1st of each month.
  3. For a one-off project, generate a separate invoice with time-logged line items and attach final deliverables as a ZIP or Google Drive link.
  4. Enable Stripe and PayPal so the client can choose their payment method; allow ACH for lower fees if available.
  5. If a payment is late, automated reminder emails send after 7 and 14 days; follow up with a friendly personal email if needed.
  6. Export monthly income reports and save them to your accounting folder for tax season.

Final thoughts and quick summary

Finding the best invoicing software for freelancers under $10/month is very possible—many capable options are free or cost just a few dollars. Wave and Zoho Invoice are excellent starting points for sole proprietors and freelancers who want strong free features. Invoice Ninja is a great choice if you need a low-cost upgrade that adds client portals and automation. Square and PayPal offer convenient, no-subscription invoicing if you prioritize payment simplicity.

Choose based on the features you actually use (client portal, recurring invoices, integrations), calculate total transaction costs, and start with a free trial when available. With the right affordable invoicing tool, you can spend less time on billing and more time on your craft—without exceeding a tight monthly budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free invoicing software for freelancers?

Several tools offer free, unlimited professional invoicing and let clients pay by card through Stripe or PayPal — enough for most low-volume freelancers.

Do freelancers really need invoicing software?

It isn't mandatory, but it gets you paid faster with automatic reminders and keeps clean records for tax time — a big time-saver over manual invoices.

Can invoicing software accept card payments?

Yes. Most tools integrate Stripe or PayPal so clients can pay a card or bank transfer directly from the invoice; a small processing fee applies.

What should a freelance invoice include?

Your name/business details, the client's details, an invoice number and date, itemised work, the total, payment terms, and your tax ID where required.

Is invoicing software worth paying for?

If you send more than a handful of invoices a month, paid tiers pay for themselves through automation, reminders, and expense/tax tracking.
