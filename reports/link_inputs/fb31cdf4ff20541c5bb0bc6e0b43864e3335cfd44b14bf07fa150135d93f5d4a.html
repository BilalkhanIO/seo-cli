Free does not always mean the same thing. In some courses, the lessons are free. In others, a badge is free, a certificate costs money, or a final exam is paid. Before you list a certificate anywhere, check what you actually earned.

What "free" can mean

A course can be free to watch, free to complete, or free only for a badge. A certificate is a separate thing. Read the provider page carefully: it should say whether lessons, a badge, a certificate, or an exam are free, paid, or both. Do not assume a free course gives a free certificate.

How to verify a certificate

First, find the certificate on the provider's own site, not only in a screenshot. Check for a verification page, a credential ID, and the name of the provider and course. Check the date and what you actually completed. If an employer or school can verify it on the provider's site, it is much stronger than a downloaded image alone.

Example: Check a Microsoft Learn Achievement

Microsoft distinguishes training achievements from credentials earned through a separate assessment. Finishing a learning module does not automatically mean you hold a professional certification. Start with the Microsoft Learn explanation of achievements and credentials, then open the record in your own Learn profile.

Write down what the issuer calls the record before choosing a label for your CV. If your record is a module achievement, describe the module you completed. If it is a credential, check the credential record and its sharing options. Do not rename one as the other.

A Verification Worksheet You Can Reuse

Make a copy of this checklist for each course. Leave a field marked “not confirmed” when the issuer does not provide the information; do not fill the gap with an assumption.

FieldWhat to record
Issuer and courseThe exact provider and course names on the official page.
What you earnedThe issuer's own label: completion record, achievement, badge or credential.
Requirements completedLessons, exercises or an assessment you actually finished.
Verification routeThe issuer's sharing or verification link, if one exists. Test what a signed-out visitor can see.

Turn the Record Into an Accurate CV Entry

Use this fill-in format: “Completed [exact course or module] from [provider], [month and year]. Practised [task you completed]. Record: [issuer sharing link, if available].” This is a writing template, not a claim that a particular course awards a certificate.

If the issuer offers no public verification page, keep the completion evidence and describe it accurately. A missing public link alone does not prove fraud, but it means you should not claim independent online verification.

Red flags to watch for

Be careful if there is no provider page, if the wording is copied from another course, if a paid exam is sold as a free certificate, or if you are asked to pay for a certificate that was advertised as free without that being clear upfront. If there is no public verification page, state what evidence you have and avoid claiming that the record is independently verifiable online.

What to put on a CV

List the course name, the provider, the date you finished, and what you actually completed — for example, the lessons and the practice tasks, or the exam if you took one. That is clearer and more honest than only writing "certificate".

FAQ

Is a free certificate worth it? It can be a good first step if you finish the practice tasks, not only watch videos. Employers usually care more about what you can do than about a single badge.
Does a badge equal a certificate? Not always. A badge can show participation, while a certificate may require an assessment or exam. Check the provider's own description.
How do employers check? Many check the provider's verification page or credential ID. Keep that link with your CV.
What if a course says free but the certificate is paid? That is common. Decide whether you need the certificate itself, or whether completing the lessons and practice is enough for your goal.
